# Blue Language — Complete Curriculum Map (A1.1–C2.2)

## A1.1

- **Unit 1 — Greetings & Introductions** — Introduce yourself, exchange basic personal information, and use the present form of be accurately. Grammar focus: Present be: am, is, are + subject pronouns.
- **Unit 2 — Family & People** — Talk about family members and ownership using possessive adjectives and possessive nouns. Grammar focus: Possessive adjectives and possessive nouns.
- **Unit 3 — Daily Routines** — Describe everyday routines using the present simple affirmative with common time expressions. Grammar focus: Present simple affirmative.
- **Unit 4 — Questions About Everyday Life** — Ask and answer simple present questions and make negative statements about routines. Grammar focus: Present simple negatives and questions with do/does.
- **Unit 5 — Home & Things** — Describe possessions and everyday objects using have and has in clear simple sentences. Grammar focus: Have and has for possession.
- **Unit 6 — Food & Everyday Choices** — Use articles and plural nouns to name common foods and make basic descriptions. Grammar focus: Articles a/an/the and regular plural nouns.
- **Unit 7 — Clothes & Descriptions** — Use demonstratives and basic adjectives to identify and describe people and things. Grammar focus: This, that, these, those + adjective position.
- **Unit 8 — Places, Time & Simple Directions** — Use basic prepositions of place and time to locate people, things, and routine events. Grammar focus: Basic prepositions of place and time.

## A1.2

- **Unit 1 — Free Time & Likes** — Talk about hobbies and how often you do familiar activities using the present simple and frequency adverbs. Grammar focus: Frequency adverbs with the present simple.
- **Unit 2 — School & Study** — Ask and answer direct questions about school subjects, homework, and classroom routines. Grammar focus: Direct Wh- questions with be and do/does.
- **Unit 3 — Shopping & Prices** — Talk about basic shopping choices, prices, and sizes using demonstratives and simple question forms. Grammar focus: Demonstratives and How much ...? with be.
- **Unit 4 — Town & Services** — Describe places in a town and say where services are using there is/are and location language. Grammar focus: There is / There are with place descriptions.
- **Unit 5 — Weather & Seasons** — Describe common weather and seasonal routines using be, present simple, and time prepositions. Grammar focus: Be + weather adjectives and in/on time phrases.
- **Unit 6 — Health & Body** — Talk about body parts and simple physical states using have/has and be. Grammar focus: Have/has for simple symptoms and be + physical-state adjectives.
- **Unit 7 — Transport & Travel Basics** — Talk about everyday transport, schedules, and journeys using the present simple and common travel expressions. Grammar focus: Present simple for routines and fixed everyday schedules.
- **Unit 8 — Everyday Communication** — Exchange basic contact information and manage simple communication problems using direct questions and present forms. Grammar focus: Basic information questions and clarification with present forms.

## A2.1

- **Unit 1 — A Weekend Away** — Describe a completed trip using common regular and irregular past-simple forms. Grammar focus: Past simple affirmative: regular and common irregular verbs.
- **Unit 2 — What Happened?** — Ask about past events and make past-simple negatives using did/didn’t. Grammar focus: Past simple negatives and questions with did.
- **Unit 3 — What Is Everyone Doing?** — Contrast actions happening now with regular routines using the present continuous and present simple. Grammar focus: Present continuous for now vs present simple for routine.
- **Unit 4 — Comparing Places** — Compare towns, neighborhoods, and destinations using comparative and superlative adjectives. Grammar focus: Comparative and superlative adjectives.
- **Unit 5 — Food & Quantities** — Talk about quantities of food and drink using countable/uncountable nouns and common quantifiers. Grammar focus: Countable and uncountable nouns with some/any/much/many.
- **Unit 6 — Plans for Next Month** — Talk about intentions and planned future actions using be going to. Grammar focus: Be going to for intentions and plans.
- **Unit 7 — Advice & Possibility** — Use can, could, and should for present ability, polite possibility, and simple advice. Grammar focus: Can, could, and should for ability, requests, possibility, and advice.
- **Unit 8 — A Story in Progress** — Combine past continuous background actions with past-simple events in a short story. Grammar focus: Past continuous and past simple together.

## A2.2

- **Unit 1 — Tasks Not Finished Yet** — Use the present perfect with yet in simple questions and negatives about expected tasks. Grammar focus: Present perfect with yet for expected completion.
- **Unit 2 — Goals & Learning Choices** — Use common verb + to-infinitive patterns to talk about goals, preferences, and decisions. Grammar focus: Common verbs followed by to-infinitive.
- **Unit 3 — Offers, Decisions & Predictions** — Use will for simple future decisions, offers, promises, and basic predictions. Grammar focus: Will for instant decisions, offers, promises, and basic predictions.
- **Unit 4 — Arrangements in the Calendar** — Use the present continuous for fixed future arrangements with a clear time or place. Grammar focus: Present continuous for future arrangements.
- **Unit 5 — Giving & Sending Things** — Use direct and indirect objects with common verbs such as give, send, show, and bring. Grammar focus: Direct and indirect objects with give/send/show/bring.
- **Unit 6 — Thoughts, Facts & Opinions** — Use simple that-clauses after think, know, believe, and hope to express opinions and information. Grammar focus: Simple that-clauses after think/know/believe/hope.
- **Unit 7 — Rules & Necessity** — Use must, mustn’t, have to, and don’t have to for simple rules and necessity. Grammar focus: Must/mustn’t and have to/don’t have to.
- **Unit 8 — Purpose & Practical Instructions** — Use the to-infinitive of purpose to explain why people use tools, websites, and everyday actions. Grammar focus: To-infinitive of purpose.

## B1.1

- **Unit 1 — Experiences & Achievements** — Talk about life experiences and achievements without naming a finished past time. Grammar focus: Present perfect for life experience: ever, never, and unspecified time.
- **Unit 2 — Changes Over Time** — Describe situations that began in the past and continue now using for and since. Grammar focus: Present perfect with for, since, and how long.
- **Unit 3 — Before the Main Event** — Show which past action happened earlier using the past perfect. Grammar focus: Past perfect for the earlier of two past events.
- **Unit 4 — Future Choices** — Compare will, be going to, and the present continuous for different future meanings. Grammar focus: Future forms: will, be going to, and present continuous.
- **Unit 5 — Making Deductions** — Use must, might, could, and can’t to express different degrees of certainty about the present. Grammar focus: Modals of deduction: must, might, could, and can’t.
- **Unit 6 — People Who Matter** — Use defining relative clauses to identify people, places, and things precisely. Grammar focus: Defining relative clauses with who, which, that, where, and whose.
- **Unit 7 — Work & Learning Decisions** — Use common gerund and infinitive patterns after verbs and expressions. Grammar focus: Gerunds and infinitives after common verbs.
- **Unit 8 — If Things Go Wrong** — Use the first conditional and unless to discuss realistic future consequences. Grammar focus: First conditional and unless for realistic future situations.

## B1.2

- **Unit 1 — Imagined Choices** — Discuss unlikely or imaginary present and future situations using the second conditional. Grammar focus: Second conditional for imaginary or unlikely situations.
- **Unit 2 — Processes & Products** — Use present and past passive forms to focus on processes, products, and results. Grammar focus: Present and past passive: be + past participle.
- **Unit 3 — Reporting What People Said** — Report statements with tense and reference changes when needed. Grammar focus: Reported statements: said/told and backshift.
- **Unit 4 — Reporting Questions & Requests** — Report yes/no questions, Wh- questions, and requests using indirect structures. Grammar focus: Reported questions and requests.
- **Unit 5 — Work in Progress** — Use the present perfect continuous to emphasize ongoing or repeated recent activity. Grammar focus: Present perfect continuous: have/has been + -ing.
- **Unit 6 — How Life Used to Be** — Describe past states and repeated habits with used to and would. Grammar focus: Used to and would for past habits.
- **Unit 7 — Checking & Agreeing** — Use question tags to check information, invite agreement, and manage conversation. Grammar focus: Question tags: auxiliary + pronoun.
- **Unit 8 — Connecting Arguments** — Use contrast and result linkers to build clear connected arguments. Grammar focus: Contrast and result linkers: however, whereas, despite, although, therefore.

## B2.1

- **Unit 1 — Narratives with Time Depth** — Control past simple, past continuous, past perfect, and past perfect continuous in a developed narrative. Grammar focus: Narrative tenses: past simple, continuous, perfect, and perfect continuous.
- **Unit 2 — Focus Through the Passive** — Use passive forms across several tenses and choose whether to mention the agent. Grammar focus: Passive across tenses and agent choice.
- **Unit 3 — Looking Back at Alternative Outcomes** — Use third conditional patterns to evaluate unreal past situations and their consequences. Grammar focus: Third conditional for unreal past conditions and results.
- **Unit 4 — Reasoning About the Past** — Use must have, might have, could have, and can’t have to evaluate past evidence. Grammar focus: Past modals of deduction: must/might/could/can’t have + past participle.
- **Unit 5 — Adding Information Precisely** — Use non-defining relative clauses to add supplementary information without changing reference. Grammar focus: Non-defining relative clauses and punctuation.
- **Unit 6 — Reporting with Attitude** — Use a wider range of reporting verbs and complement patterns to represent stance accurately. Grammar focus: Reporting verbs and complement patterns.
- **Unit 7 — Meaning Changes with Verb Patterns** — Distinguish common verbs whose meaning changes with a gerund or infinitive complement. Grammar focus: Gerund vs infinitive after remember, stop, try, and regret.
- **Unit 8 — Planning from a Future Viewpoint** — Use future continuous and future perfect to describe activity and completion from a future reference point. Grammar focus: Future continuous and future perfect.

## B2.2

- **Unit 1 — Arranging Services** — Use have/get + object + past participle to describe services and actions arranged for you. Grammar focus: Causative have/get: have/get + object + past participle.
- **Unit 2 — Past Choices, Present Results** — Use mixed conditionals to connect unreal past causes with present results and present conditions with past outcomes. Grammar focus: Mixed conditionals across past and present.
- **Unit 3 — Wishes About Reality** — Use wish and if only to express dissatisfaction, regret, and desired change across time. Grammar focus: Wish and if only: present dissatisfaction, past regret, and desired change.
- **Unit 4 — Compressing Information** — Use participle clauses to express time, reason, condition, and result more concisely. Grammar focus: Participle clauses for concise relationships.
- **Unit 5 — What Should Have Been Done** — Use modal passive and perfect passive forms for rules, expectations, criticism, and past possibility. Grammar focus: Modal passive and modal perfect passive.
- **Unit 6 — Precision with Nouns & Quantities** — Use articles, zero article, uncountable nouns, and quantifiers with greater precision. Grammar focus: Articles, zero article, uncountable nouns, and quantifiers.
- **Unit 7 — Signposting Complex Arguments** — Use a wider range of discourse markers to show addition, contrast, consequence, and qualification. Grammar focus: Discourse markers for addition, contrast, consequence, and qualification.
- **Unit 8 — Careful Claims & Hedging** — Use hedging and stance language to make appropriately cautious B2 claims. Grammar focus: Hedging and stance: modals, adverbs, and cautious reporting.

## C1.1

- **Unit 1 — Evidence, Uncertainty & Qualified Claims** — Evaluate evidence and calibrate claims with advanced epistemic stance language. Grammar focus: Epistemic stance and calibrated claims.
- **Unit 2 — Inversion for Restriction & Emphasis** — Use negative and restrictive adverbials with inversion to create controlled emphasis. Grammar focus: Inversion after negative and restrictive adverbials.
- **Unit 3 — Clefts, Fronting & Information Focus** — Reshape sentence structure to foreground contrast, correction, and key information. Grammar focus: Clefts and fronting for information focus.
- **Unit 4 — Participle Clauses & Concise Relations** — Use participle clauses to compress related information without creating ambiguity. Grammar focus: Participle clauses and reduced adverbial relations.
- **Unit 5 — Advanced Relative Reference** — Control complex relative clauses, prepositional relatives, and sentential reference. Grammar focus: Advanced relative clauses and sentential reference.
- **Unit 6 — Reporting Verbs & Source Distance** — Use reporting verbs to represent stance, evidence, and distance from a source claim. Grammar focus: Reporting verbs, complementation and source stance.
- **Unit 7 — Nominalisation & Formal Academic Style** — Use nominalisation strategically while preserving readability and agency. Grammar focus: Nominalisation, agency and formal information packaging.
- **Unit 8 — Inverted & Mixed Conditionals** — Use advanced conditional inversion and mixed time reference for hypothetical reasoning. Grammar focus: Inverted conditionals and mixed time reference.

## C1.2

- **Unit 1 — Past Modality: Obligation, Deduction & Regret** — Distinguish should have, needn’t have, might have, and related modal-perfect meanings. Grammar focus: Modal perfects for past obligation, deduction and possibility.
- **Unit 2 — Concession Beyond Although** — Use advanced concessive structures to acknowledge limits without losing the main argument. Grammar focus: Advanced concession and contrast.
- **Unit 3 — Ellipsis & Substitution** — Use ellipsis and substitution to avoid repetition while keeping reference recoverable. Grammar focus: Ellipsis and substitution in connected discourse.
- **Unit 4 — Emphasis, Degree & Exclamative Patterns** — Control emphatic auxiliaries, degree structures, and exclamatives without sounding exaggerated. Grammar focus: Emphasis with auxiliaries, degree and exclamative structures.
- **Unit 5 — Impersonal Reporting Passives** — Use impersonal and personal reporting passives to attribute information with appropriate distance. Grammar focus: Impersonal and personal reporting passives.
- **Unit 6 — Complex Noun Phrases** — Build and unpack dense noun phrases while controlling scope and readability. Grammar focus: Complex noun phrases and modifier scope.
- **Unit 7 — Cohesion, Reference & Text Flow** — Track reference across sentences using advanced cohesive devices without obscuring meaning. Grammar focus: Advanced cohesion and reference tracking.
- **Unit 8 — Register, Politeness & Mitigation** — Adjust requests, disagreement, and recommendations to hierarchy, relationship, and context. Grammar focus: Register, mitigation and interpersonal stance.

## C2.1

- **Unit 1 — Evidentiality & Degrees of Commitment** — Calibrate evidential source, certainty, and commitment at proficient level. Grammar focus: Evidentiality and fine-grained commitment.
- **Unit 2 — Marked Inversion & Rhetorical Architecture** — Use marked inversion as a deliberate rhetorical resource rather than ornamental syntax. Grammar focus: Marked inversion as rhetorical architecture.
- **Unit 3 — Pragmatic Conditionals & Parenthetical Meaning** — Use conditionals that organize discourse, soften claims, or frame relevance rather than express literal conditions. Grammar focus: Pragmatic conditionals and discourse functions of if.
- **Unit 4 — Advanced Aspect & Temporal Viewpoint** — Manipulate viewpoint, unrealized intention, and temporal layering across complex narratives. Grammar focus: Aspect, prospective-in-the-past and unrealized intention.
- **Unit 5 — Logical Nuance in Discourse Relations** — Choose connectors that encode precise logical relations across dense arguments. Grammar focus: Fine-grained discourse relations and advanced connectors.
- **Unit 6 — High-Level Collocation & Phraseological Control** — Use advanced collocations and lexical bundles with natural constraint and precision. Grammar focus: Collocational and phraseological control.
- **Unit 7 — Figurative Meaning, Connotation & Allusion** — Interpret figurative language through context, cultural knowledge, and rhetorical purpose. Grammar focus: Figurative language, connotation and interpretive stance.
- **Unit 8 — Mediation, Reformulation & Audience Design** — Reformulate complex information for a new audience while preserving nuance and evidential status. Grammar focus: Mediation, reformulation and audience-sensitive paraphrase.

## C2.2

- **Unit 1 — Diplomatic Disagreement & Register Calibration** — Disagree, qualify, and challenge with precise control of interpersonal force. Grammar focus: Diplomatic disagreement and calibrated interpersonal force.
- **Unit 2 — Implicature, Presupposition & Context** — Interpret implied meaning, presupposed information, and context-dependent reference. Grammar focus: Implicature, presupposition and context-dependent meaning.
- **Unit 3 — Dense Academic Prose & Information Packaging** — Control nominal style, embedding, and information density without sacrificing interpretability. Grammar focus: Dense information packaging and clausal unpacking.
- **Unit 4 — Counterfactual Reasoning & Causal Models** — Reason precisely about unrealized alternatives without confusing correlation, necessity, and sufficiency. Grammar focus: Counterfactual conditionals and causal reasoning.
- **Unit 5 — Evidential Reporting & Source Provenance** — Represent firsthand, secondhand, documentary, and uncorroborated evidence with exact source language. Grammar focus: Evidential reporting, provenance and corroboration language.
- **Unit 6 — Rhetorical Patterning & Controlled Persuasion** — Use parallelism, antithesis, and rhetorical patterning with restraint and argumentative purpose. Grammar focus: Rhetorical parallelism, antithesis and patterned emphasis.
- **Unit 7 — Editing for Concision, Precision & Rhythm** — Revise advanced prose by removing redundancy, improving structure, and preserving nuance. Grammar focus: Advanced editing: concision, precision and rhythm.
- **Unit 8 — Integrated Synthesis & Cross-Source Evaluation** — Synthesize multiple sources, reconcile tensions, and produce audience-specific conclusions. Grammar focus: Cross-source synthesis, reconciliation and audience-specific conclusion.
